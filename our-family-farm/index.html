Our family’s story on Tubbs Road began with a small dairy farm. The Christmas trees came later, when Ronald Granger saw that the farm needed a new direction.

Our family’s story

In 1969, Ronald and Esther Granger bought a small dairy farm from relatives in Mexico, New York. They planned to keep milking cows, but as more small dairies closed, Ronald could see the choice ahead: grow the dairy to keep it going, or find a different way to farm the land.

Ronald chose to grow Christmas trees. He and his son Randy planted their first trees in the early 1980s and spent years preparing and tending the fields. That work paid off in the winter of 1997, when the family sold its first Christmas tree. Ronald taught his sons how to care for the land and keep the tree fields in shape, passing on what he’d learned from years of working the farm.

Ronald and his two sons grew the farm together. When it passed to Randy and Ramey Granger, they carried on the work Ronald and Esther had started.

Randy and Ray grew up milking cows alongside their father, Ronald. Ray went on to learn how to build houses and other structures, while Randy got to know the land on Tubbs Road and what it could grow. That first season, the family worked out of a small shed across the road from the old farmhouse, right beside the heifer barn. They started small and built on what they had each year. Ray built and maintained the buildings the farm needed as it grew, and Randy used what he’d learned about the land to grow Christmas trees and, eventually, pumpkins. You could see the years of hard work Ronald, Randy, and Ray put in, from the rows of Christmas trees to the buildings that went up as the farm grew.

Esther ran the farm kitchen, and her sweet and sassy personality kept people coming back. To the day she died, she’d tell you her fudge was the best. People still ask for it today.

Today, the same care goes into our Christmas trees and pumpkins. Our Nigerian dwarf goats have become part of life here, too.

The original Hallmark movie started right here on Tubbs Road. Andrea, Randy’s oldest daughter, grew up working alongside her family on the farm. She spent a few years in the city learning how businesses run, then brought what she learned back home. Hallmark took that plot and ran with it. Now she’s putting down deeper roots, learning from her father and uncle as she prepares to lead the farm into its third generation and keep what her grandparents started going for the generations to come.

We’re glad to share this place with your family, whether you’re visiting for the first time or coming back for a tradition of your own.

How we grow your Christmas tree

Long before you walk the rows looking for your tree, it starts as a little seedling in one of our fields. A Christmas tree can take seven to ten years to reach a size that’s ready to take home. In the same field, you’ll see little trees just getting started and taller ones that are nearly ready for Christmas.

A little care, year after year

We prepare the ground before we plant and keep the grass and weeds down while the young trees put down roots. Every growing season, we mow between the rows. Once the trees are old enough, we trim their new growth each year to encourage fuller branches and help shape them. As mature trees are cut, there’s room to plant another generation. More on growing Christmas trees from Cornell Cooperative Extension.

A real tree after Christmas

While they grow, Christmas trees absorb carbon dioxide and can provide shelter for wildlife. A real tree is made of wood and needles that break down naturally. Once the decorations and tinsel are off, a tree recycling program can turn it into mulch. When you buy a tree from a local farm, you help support the people who plant and tend those fields year after year. More on real Christmas trees from Penn State Extension.
